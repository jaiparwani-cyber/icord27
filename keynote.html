  • Keynote 1: Professor Joshua D. Summers, University of Texas at Dallas, USA

    Keynote 1: Professor Joshua D. Summers, University of Texas at Dallas, USA



    Short Bio

    Joshua D. Summers is Head and Professor of Mechanical Engineering at the University of Texas at Dallas, joining in 2021.  Dr. Summers earned his Ph.D. from ASU (design automation) and his MS (submarine design) and BS (fluidized bed design) from University of Missouri.  He has worked at the Naval Research Laboratory (VR Lab and NCARAI).  He was formerly a Professor at Clemson University (2002-2020).  Dr. Summers’ research has been funded by government, large industry, and small-medium sized enterprises.  His areas of interest include collaborative design, knowledge management, and design enabler development with the overall objective of improving design through collaboration and computation.  Dr. Summers’ greatest contributions are made through his mentoring of graduate students and other colleagues – developing people is his true passion.

    Title of the keynote

    Design Complexity – Measuring It to Exploit It

    Abstract

    Complexity is often considered the “big bad” for engineering design.  The most common design principle is to “keep it simple” as complex systems tend to increase the cost of the product, decreases the useability and maintainability, increases the potential for failure, or increases the time to market.  Essentially, complexity is often a surrogate for many other requirements and measures of goodness for design.  Thus, we want to be able to measure complexity of a design problem, process, or product to provide insights into these other measures that are of true interest.  In this keynote, we will look at how we can measure complexity (size, coupling, and solvability).  We will show how to use these metrics of complexity to predict product performance values that have apparently no direct or strong relationship to the models explored.  This is done by using multiple complexity measures to train a crowd of artificial neural networks (ANN Crowd) to predict market price from function structures, for instance.  Ultimately, the case is made to not try to integrate complexity into a single metric, but to consider multiple points of view to expose deeper patterns and meanings in early stage design representations.

    P. V. Madhusudhan Rao is professor in departments of mechanical engineering and design. He is also founding head of the department of design.     Title of the keynote

    Challenges in Designing for Visual Impairment

    Abstract

    In this information driven era, the use of pictorial forms of communication have become widely popular but at the same time making the world increasingly inaccessible to the visually impaired and blind. The current product design, communication design and interaction design efforts are not inclusive enough to allow visually challenged people to have same usability and experience as sighted people. This talk will bring out some real challenges for designers to come up with assistive products/solutions/services which will allow visually challenged people to be independent and facilitate their participation in activities of daily living.

    Title of the keynote

    Typography Research in Indian Scripts

    Abstract

    Typography is a means of communication through letterforms. In Communication Design, it is an important subject to learn and master to create an effective communication. Research into this discipline is often based on Latin script. Unlike Western Typography which is well documented and researched, there is huge dearth of research in Typography of Indian scripts. India being a land of multi-script and languages has a wealth of untapped research opportunities to be discovered. This paper presents few of the researches done in Indian scripts. It further highlights the need for extensive research and resource development in Indian Typography.

    Keywords: Typography, Indian Scripts, Communication Design, Letterforms

  • Keynote 4: Professor Lucienne Blessing, Singapore University of Technology and Design, Singapore

    Keynote 4: Professor Luciënne T. M. Blessing, Singapore University of Technology and Design, Singapore



    Short Bio

    Lucienne held research, teaching and management positions at: Singapore University of Technology and Design; University of Luxembourg; University of the Greater Region; Berlin Technical University; Cambridge University, and University of Twente. She has a MSc from Delft University of Technology, PhD from University of Twente and Honorary Doctorate from Mälardalen University, Sweden. Her research includes: design processes and theories, design education, design science, user experience, transdisciplinarity, and ethics. She co-founded the Design Society, was Editor-in-Chief (Europe) of Research in Engineering Design; and translated (with Ken Wallace) Engineering Design of Pahl and Beitz.

    Title of the keynote

    Industry X.0 – Design X.0 – Research X.0

    Abstract

    Advantages in technology have given industry and society tremendous opportunities as well as enormous challenges, many of which still have not been solved or even addressed. Digital transformation at all levels is a must, but is Smart really smart? The recent global crises - COVID and climate change - and the societal debates about data capture and use, have shown the fragility of the concept of connectivity underlying Industry 4.0, 5.0 and 6.0. Design, by its nature, could lay the foundation for an economically, socially, and environmentally successful industrial revolution, provided the availability of the required knowledge, methods and tools. The presentation will discuss the three industrial revolutions, highlight areas that could benefit from a design approach, and the areas that urgently require design research.

  • Keynote 5: Mr Apoorv Shankar, Ultrahuman, India

    Keynote 5: Mr Apoorv Shankar, Ultrahuman, India



    Short Bio

    Apoorv Shankar is the VP of Hardware at Ultrahuman, a Consumer Health tech startup based in Bangalore and the UAE building metabolic health-focused wearable products including a CGM patch and a Smart ring. Ultrahuman has become a well known brand in the Health and fitness space in the last few months counting a lot of Cricketers and Bollywood celebrities as their customers.

    Apoorv completed his M.Des(in Product Design and Engineering) at CPDM, IISc in 2017 after which he co-founded LazyCo wherein he was building Aina, an AI-powered smart ring to make day-to-day interaction with the smartphone more efficient, the company won several awards for the product and in 2021, LazyCo was acquired by Ultrahuman.

    Title of the keynote

    Design Driven Innovation vs Human-Centered Design

    Abstract

    As structured methods of innovation, both design-driven and human/user-centered approaches have their own merits. In the last decade, Design-driven innovation has become very popular as compared to other innovation methods, and large design-focused consumer companies like Apple, Airbnb have been able to create radical innovations in their respective fields multiple times using such methods repeatedly. As a designer or innovator working in RnD, when should one follow the principles of Design driven innovations as compared to other innovation methods based on recent case studies are going to be explored in this talk.
