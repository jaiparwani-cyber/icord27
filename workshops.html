  • Workshop 3: Innovation and Design Engineering: Emerging Contextualization in Affordable Product Development

    Workshop 3: Innovation and Design Engineering: Emerging Contextualization in Affordable Product Development

    Title: Innovation and Design Engineering: Emerging Contextualization in Affordable Product Development

    Time: Day 3 | 11 January 2023 | IST:1330-1430| Parallel Session 3

    Name and affiliation of the Workshop Chair(s): Dr Pranab K Dan, Associate Professor, Rajendra Mishra School of Engineering Entrepreneurship, Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur, India

    Abstract: This workshop on Innovation and Design Engineering is purposed to familiarize and edify on an emerging paradigm of innovation engineering for affordable product design and realization along with its related approaches. Affordable innovation oftentimes creates disruptive solutions and such engineering strategy leads to reverse innovation. Innovation and design engineering fosters improvement in extant technologies, systems & infrastructure, and resources to develop a completely new concept or enhance productivity and cost-effectiveness across the entire process. Innovation engineering as a contextual paradigm for creating value-sensitive designs and products is gaining momentum as products built with affordability engineering and with copacetic quality and reliability are finding strong demand in the markets of emerging economies as well as certain segments in the developed countries, comprising two-thirds of world population, acting as the driver of the economy. The discussion will be around the principles and methodologies that consider design at the core for affordable innovation, highlighting several applications and example cases including med-tech and automotive sectors. The workshop will adopt pedagogy comprising lecture, short case analysis, and presentation exercise. It is aimed to inculcate a sense of priority in innovation and design engineering based frugal or affordability paradigm, to cater to an expanding market.

    Outcomes of the workshop: This workshop course will help the attendees to:

    • Develop an appreciation of the paradigm of innovation engineering and design in emerging economies as well as reverse innovation for developed countries covering a large market of five billion people

    • Enable understanding of the concepts of innovation engineering and affordable design

    • Comprehend the principles and processes involved in innovation engineering

    • Build competency to apply the methodologies of innovation engineering.

    Who should participate: Academics, practitioners, and research students from the areas of design, engineering, innovation and manufacturing, and related areas.

  • Workshop 4/10: How to build the SAPPhIRE model of causality representing the working of engineering systems

    Workshop 4: How to build the SAPPhIRE model of causality representing the working of engineering systems

    Title: How to build the SAPPhIRE model of causality representing the working of engineering systems

    Time: Day 3 | 11 January 2023 | IST:1330-1530| Parallel Session 4

    Name and affiliation of the Workshop Chair(s): Kausik Bhattacharya, PhD Researcher, IISc, Anubhab Majumdar, PhD Researcher, IISc, and Apoorv N. Bhatt, PhD Researcher, IISc

    Abstract: The SAPPhIRE model of causality is a powerful representation of the working of engineering and biological systems. They are found to be useful in many design activities, such as creative ideation, functional reasoning, assessment of creativity, and process analysis. However, representing a system using a SAPPhIRE model requires expertise and experience. We developed a guideline to help researchers with no prior experience in SAPPhIRE to create the SAPPhIRE model of the working of engineering systems using knowledge given in technical documents of natural languages. In this workshop, the participants will be taught the SAPPhIRE model of causality and how to create one using the guideline document. Participants will experience hands-on learning of SAPPhIRE modeling with live examples. The data collected from this workshop will be used to measure the effectiveness of this guideline document and identify opportunities for future improvement. Here is a broad timeline of the workshop:

    • Opening, review of workshop objectives

    • Overview of SAPPhIRE model of causality, Instructions on how to create SAPPhIRE model using knowledge given in natural language text

    • Participants Create SAPPhIRE model of two examples

    • Workshop summary and wrap-up

    Outcomes of the workshop: (a) Participants of this workshop will learn about how to create the SAPPhIRE representation of engineering systems using information available in natural language text (b) IISc research team will collect data on SAPPhIRE modelling for analysis and further improvement at the later stage.

    Who should participate: a) Design researchers, (b) Design practitioners, and (c) Design Students

  • Workshop 9: Interactive Qualitative Analysis - Context of concept design exploration

    Workshop 9:Interactive Qualitative Analysis - Context of concept design exploration

    Title: Interactive Qualitative Analysis - Context of concept design exploration

    Time: Day 3 | 11 January 2023 | IST:1430-1530| Parallel Session 3

    Name and affiliation of the Workshop Chair(s): Dr Ananthavalli Ramesh

    Abstract: IQA is a qualitative research methodology. The systematic approach of divergence and convergence process is one of the recent development in explorative research methods. The research flow has four distinct phases: research design, focus group, interview and report. It uses the focus groups to identify the ‘affinities’ of a system that will ultimately represent the group’s experience with the phenomenon. The group identifies the relationships between each of the affinities. ‘Mind Map’ of the group’s reality establishes the system map. Affinities defined by the group lead to development of a protocol for interviews. Meanings of affinities and their systemic relationships are further explored during interview process. A comprehensive system diagram is developed from the interviews to explain the phenomenon. The final part enables the researcher to describe the affinities and their relationships.

    Outcomes of the workshop: Based on the grounded theory approach, IQA facilitates the researchers to capture the untapped knowledge resources. This can be applied to any given scenario especially in exploring customer pain points, journey map and customer engagement process.

    Who should participate: Research scholars, teaching professions and industry professionals who deals with design and exploration process.

  • Workshop 12: Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) for Quantifying Environmental Sustainability of Products and Systems

    Workshop 12: Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) for Quantifying Environmental Sustainability of Products and Systems

    Title: Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) for Quantifying Environmental Sustainability of Products and Systems

    Time: Day 3 | 11 January 2023 | IST: 1430-1530 | Parallel Session 6

    Name and affiliation of the Workshop Chair(s): Ishaan Kaushal, PhD Researcher, IISc Bangalore

    Abstract: The workshop will focus on the introduction to Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) for designers, business planners, and sustainability enthusiasts. Specifically, from the designer’s perspective, a lot of decisions about the product life cycle are taken during the design phase, and it is important that informed decisions in regard to sustainability are made by the designers at this early phase and LCA can help designers make such decisions. LCA is a method for quantitatively assessing a broad range of environmental aspects and potential impacts associated with a product, process, or service by taking a life cycle perspective from raw material acquisition through production (cradle to gate), use, end-of-life recovery, and disposal (cradle to grave). LCA helps decision-makers to identify the solutions that best support sustainable development.

    The workshop intends to impart a hands-on start to LCA, with an introduction to LCA, LCI data, Sustainability claims, Circular economy, and an introduction to the associated standards landscape. The workshop assumes no previous knowledge of LCA. After the workshop, participants will have a fair idea of Sustainability and will be able to bring Life Cycle thinking into their own domains.  

    Broad timeline:  

    • Introduction to LCA and associated standards (25 mins) 

    • Modelling an LCA case study (25 mins) 

    • Q&A 

    • Take home Assignment 

    Outcomes of the workshop: The attendees will get introduced to the LCA method and associated standards, with hands-on exposure to openLCA software.

    Who should participate:

    • Product and System Designers

    • Business Developers

    • Operations and Supply Chain Engineers

    • NGOs

    • Sustainability Enthusiasts, in general

  • Workshop 14: Status of Systems Thinking in Design- Theoretical and Practical Aspects

    Workshop 14: Status of Systems Thinking in Design- Theoretical and Practical Aspects

    Title: Status of Systems Thinking in Design- Theoretical and Practical Aspects

    Time: Day 3 | 11 January 2023 | IST: 1600-1700| Parallel Session 2

    Name and affiliation of the Workshop Chair(s): Vishal Singh, Associate Professor, Indian Institute of Science

    Abstract: Systems Thinking is a holistic approach that allows making sense of the complexity of the problem and the context in terms of the interdependencies and relationships at different levels. With increasing recognition of the complexity of the challenges humanity and society faces at the systems level, such as climate change and the ageing population, there is growing interest in finding holistic solutions through design interventions that leverage Systems Thinking. Consequently, there is growing interest in Systems Thinking in design. However, Systems Thinking is not a new concept. There is diverse literature and discussion on Systems Thinking and its variants across different domains. More importantly, there is a noticeable gap in the theoretical discussion on Systems Thinking and its application in practice.

    Consequently, any attempt to integrate Systems Thinking approaches in design, design thinking, and design practice must build on a sound theoretical and pragmatic understanding of the advancements in Systems Thinking. Therefore, this workshop provides a forum to review and discuss the status of Systems Thinking in design. This moderated workshop will use a structured framework for the review and discussion covering seminal works on Systems Thinking and connecting the discussions with other thinking paradigms, including Design Thinking and Computational Thinking.

    Outcomes of the workshop: Following are the key outcomes and benefits that the participants can expect from the workshop:

    • A summary of the workshop discussions will be shared with the workshop participants as a follow-up activity

    • If there is collective interest towards creating a Special Interest Group (SIG) on the topic, the workshop attendance will provide the initial momentum for such a collective effort.

    • The workshop can provide the initial background and directions for those curious about the topic, but with a limited existing background in the specifics.

    • For those with prior interest and background in the topic, the workshop will provide an opportunity to

      • Exchange knowledge, experiences and ideas with others interested in the topic

      • Critique the discussed framework and topics on Systems Thinking in Design

      • Identify potential collaborators and specific sub-areas of research interest to build on collectively

    Who should participate: The workshop is open to all registered delegates of the ICoRD'23 Conference. However, we would like to cap the number of participants to 30 for effective group work and interaction within the stipulated time.

    Name and affiliation of the Workshop Chair(s): Dr. Shakuntala Acharya, IIT Guwahati & Mr. Apoorv Naresh Bhatt, IISc Bangalore

    Abstract: Problem-based learning (PBL) has profound implications on the motivations of the student to learn and is known to help develop critical thinking, complex problem- solving, self-learning, collaboration and communication skills, thereby enabling fresh graduates to be industry-ready. Problem-based learning is an “instructional (and curricular) learner-centered approach that empowers learners to conduct research, integrate theory and practice, and apply knowledge and skills to develop a viable solution to a defined problem”, in which students learn through “facilitated problem solving that centers on a complex problem that does not have a single correct answer”.     The objective of this workshop is to equip the particiapnt with a selection of theories, practical tools and realworld case examples to develop their pedagogical confidence, framed by problem-based learning approach.

  • Workshop 18: Unleashing Creativity through Empathy

    Workshop 18: Unleashing Creativity through Empathy

    Title: Unleashing Creativity through Empathy

    Time: Day 3 | 11 January 2023 | IST: 1600-1700| Parallel Session 6

    Name and affiliation of the Workshop Chair(s): Md Haseen Akhtar, Department of Design, IIT Kanpur

    Abstract: The workshop will revolve around empathy as a core part of the design activity. The participants will be exposed on how to use empathy to unleash creativity in their design activities. The workshop will run in sprint mode, where there will be an introduction of the workshop for the first 10 minutes, followed by a quick exercise of empathy inside the campus with a tailored on-spot problem statement. Then the participants will be divided into groups of three (this will depend on the number of participants) to brainstorm ideas based on the sprint activity they did in the first 10 for the next 10. 30 minutes gone in a flash!

    The following 10 minutes will be again a repetition of the empathy activity but with a twist. The user will be interchanged among the team members. This is to teach the value of observation, to question, listen, and transfer the needs and aspirations of the users as close as possible to the team members for further brainstorming. The following 10 minutes will be spent transferring the data collected onto the drawing board and finalizing ideas. The last 10 minutes will be a presentation from the teams individually to all. A 60-90-second sprint pitch/explanation.

    Creativity Unleashed!

    Outcomes of the workshop: The value of observation, questioning, listening, teamwork, and how to unleash creativity through empathy.

    Who should participate: Anyone. More focus will be on new designers/design researchers.
