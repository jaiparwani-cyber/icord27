  • ICONNN Keynote 1: Professor Uday Athavankar, MIT School of Design, Pune, India

    ICONNN Keynote 1: Professor Uday Athavankar, MIT School of Design, Pune, India



    Short Bio

    Alumnus of Sir JJ College of Architecture, Athavankar worked as an architect before switching over to Industrial Design in 1970. He later studied Industrial Design at Institute of Design, Illinois Institute of Technology, Chicago. For 50 years, he has been actively involved in design education, design research and design related consulting to several companies. He has extensively published and lectured nationally and internationally and is on the advisory boards of select international research journals. While continuing with research, he also maintains his involvement as design consultant to varied projects. Athavankar retired from IDC school of design, IIT Bombay in 2019. Earlier, he has served as Head, and later as a Dean, IIT Bombay. He is now a chief mentor to MIT School of design, Alandi, Pune. His diverse research areas include how designers think and use spatial intelligence, role of visualization and mind’s eye, product semantics and meaning making by people and cultures, bicultural objects and more recently, game design.

    Title of the keynote

    Pit Stops on Route to Designerly Thinking

    Abstract

    This is a two-part presentation. First part is concerned with how people think and take visual and functional decisions about the objects that they buy and use. It would touch professionals (Architects and designers), children, users and even game players and their thinking. Most of this work is informed by theories in cognitive psychology. This includes the work on categorization theory and on visual information processing, spatial intelligence, visual thinking and the curious role of the mind's eye. Reflecting back, these diverse explorations seem to have served three purposes. It contributed to the design knowledge. It assisted me as a faculty in explaining the logic of design decisions to the students. Lastly, it helped me create tools to assist other designers solve problems.

    The second part is a crazy reaction to my lifelong research approach. It is also an effort to escape the limitations of theory led thinking that constrained the designer in me. In that role, I had this urge to change the world of objects and buildings. I find the obsession with validation too inhibiting. Lately, I have been starting projects with unusual metaphors to escape the limiting effects of logical research based approach. Now, it is freak out first and then explore if the ideas can have a theoretical or experimental validation. The case studies will show how this is lot more designerly. Reflecting back, this journey had several pit stops, each changed the way I approach research. So, the presentation is structured around these pit stops. I plan to conclude it by comparing the approaches to explore potential directions for design research.

  • ICONNN Keynote 2: Professor Farrokh Mistree, University of Oklahoma, USA

    ICONNN Keynote 2: Professor Farrokh Mistree, University of Oklahoma, USA



    Short Bio

    Farrokh Mistree holds the L. A. Comp Chair in the School of Aerospace and Mechanical Engineering at the University of Oklahoma in Norman, Oklahoma, USA. Farrokh co-directs the Systems Realization Laboratory at OU with his wife Professor Janet K. Allen. Their research focus is on defining the emerging frontier of evolving cyber-physical-social systems when the computational models are incomplete and inaccurate. Farrokh has co-authored two textbooks, five research monographs and more than 500 technical papers dealing with the design of materials, mechanical, thermal and structural systems; ships and aircraft; engineered supply networks. Farrokh is a Distinguished Alumnus of IIT Kharagpur, a Fellow of ASME and an Associate Fellow of AIAA.

    Title of the keynote

    Design Engineering in the Age of Industry 4.0 From Make to DESIGN and Make

    Abstract

    Several countries are known for their manufacturing prowess but not for their prowess in Design AND Make. In the context of the Design AND Make construct I will highlight key principles embodied in the Industry 4.0 construct and introduce the Design Engineering 4.0 construct. Foundational to sustaining the Design AND Make construct is basic research associated with design engineering. I will end by identifying some promising research issues in realizing Design Engineering 4.0 and thence the Design and Make construct.

ICONNN Awards

Design, in its ubiquitous and inter-disciplinary sense, is one of the fastest growing areas of research, education and practice. However, while there are numerous awards for practitioners of design (i.e. designers), there are very few awards to acknowledge and honour outstanding researchers and educators of design. ICONNN awards are a small step to addressing this gap.

Starting from the year 2015, the biennial International Conference series on Research into Design (ICoRD), the first and currently the largest international conference in India in this area, has introduced two biennial awards in the area of design research and education. The awards are called ICONNN Award (or in brief ICON Award), where the acronym ICONNN  (pronounced as the word ‘icon’ is pronounced) stands for: ICoRD Outstanding Contribution to desigN scieNce and educatioN. The awards are open to individuals from any country, and are to be given at each ICoRD to two individuals who have made outstanding contributions to the advancement of research and/or education in the area of design. The past awardees include Professor John Gero of George Mason University, Professor Amit Ray of Shiv Nadar University, Prof Cees de Bont of Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Professor Sudhakar Nadkarni of Welinkar Institute of Management, Professor Imre Horvath of Delft University of Technology, and Professor TS Mruthyunjaya of Indian Institute of Science.

ICoRD'23 Organizing Committee plans to invite the awardees to attend the relevant ICoRD so as to be felicitated IN-PERSON, subject to fulfilment of the following conditions:

  • ICONNN awards will be conferred only on those, selected by the Decision Committee, who agree, and are able, to attend the relevant ICORD to be felicitated in person. No ICONNN award will be conferred in absentia.
  • Each ICONNN awardee will be invited to, and expected to deliver, an ICONNN keynote in an area of his/her choice at the relevant ICoRD.

For ICoRD’23, the ICONNN award will comprise the following:

  • Travel support to attend ICoRD’23 on a reimbursement basis: For awardees from South Asia, it is INR 40,000 (about USD500); from Americas, INR 160,000 (about USD2000); from Europe, Africa, Oceania INR 120,000 (about USD1500); from all other countries it is INR 80,000 (about USD1000).
  • Local hospitality for attending the ICoRD’23 (transfer between Bengaluru International Airport and IISc Guest House; and food and accommodation at IISc Guest House for five days including the three days of ICoRD’23 i.e. 9-11 January 2023).
  • A personalised Citation and a memento designed exclusively for ICONNN award.
  • Registration waiver to attend ICoRD’23.
